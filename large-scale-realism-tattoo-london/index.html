Composition first

One body area. One visual system.

Large realism works best when the entire area is considered before the first session. I plan the main focal point, supporting subjects, negative space and direction of movement together, so the tattoo still reads from several metres away and makes sense when you move.

A sleeve does not need every centimetre filled with detail. In practice, controlled quiet areas often make the important objects stronger. The goal is enough information to feel rich without turning the whole arm into visual noise.

Projects can be built in black and grey, colour realism, or a carefully planned combination.

01 · Hierarchy

Choose the hero first

The largest or most meaningful subject gets priority. Secondary objects support it rather than compete at the same scale.

02 · Flow

Design around anatomy

Shoulder caps, elbow transitions, forearm rotation and muscle lines affect where faces, eyes and hard edges should sit.

03 · Distance

Protect readability

Large work needs a strong silhouette and contrast map before micro-detail. Fine detail matters more when the big shapes already work.

Planning the sessions

Large work is a sequence, not one marathon.

Before the first session

Send the body area, approximate coverage, reference subjects and clear photos of any existing tattoos. I use that information to judge scale and whether an in-person consultation would improve the plan.

During the project

Sessions can run up to 7 hours. We work through the composition in stages so each session fits the larger plan rather than forcing unrelated finished fragments into the sleeve.

Existing tattoos

Old work can sometimes be integrated or covered, but that changes the composition. For cover-up areas I need clear daylight photos before I promise a direction. See cover-up information.

Large Scale Realism FAQ

What counts as a large-scale realism tattoo?+

Large-scale work uses a substantial body area and is planned as one connected composition rather than a collection of unrelated small pieces. Full sleeves, large upper-arm projects, legs, backs and multi-session extensions are typical examples.

How many sessions does a realism sleeve take?+

A full sleeve is a multi-session project. The estimate changes with colour versus black and grey, detail density, existing tattoos, anatomy and coverage. I give a project-specific estimate after reviewing the concept and placement.

Can several subjects work in one sleeve?+

Yes, if they share a clear hierarchy, compatible lighting and transitions. Two same-size focal subjects competing on the same visual plane usually weaken the composition, so I prioritise one hero and build the rest around it.

Can large realism be colour or black and grey?+

Yes. I work in both. The palette and session plan change, but body flow, scale, contrast and focal hierarchy remain the foundation.

How much does a large realism tattoo cost?+

My hourly rate is £140. I estimate larger work after reviewing your idea, placement, size, skin, references and any existing tattoos. You are charged for the actual tattooing time used.
