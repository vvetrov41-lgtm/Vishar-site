Composition first

One body area. One visual system.

Large realism works best when the entire area is considered before the first session. I plan the main focal point, supporting subjects, negative space and direction of movement together.

A sleeve does not need every centimetre filled with detail. Controlled quiet areas often make the important objects stronger and protect readability from a normal viewing distance.

Projects can be built in black and grey, colour realism, or a carefully planned combination.

01 · Hierarchy

Choose the hero first

The largest or most meaningful subject gets priority. Secondary objects support it rather than compete at the same scale.

02 · Flow

Design around anatomy

Shoulder caps, elbow transitions, forearm rotation and muscle lines affect where faces, eyes and hard edges should sit.

03 · Distance

Protect readability

Large work needs a strong silhouette and contrast map before micro-detail. Fine detail matters more when the big shapes already work.

Large Scale FAQ

What counts as a large-scale realism tattoo?

Full sleeves, substantial upper-arm projects, legs, backs and other multi-session compositions designed as one connected piece.

How many sessions does a realism sleeve take?

The answer depends on coverage, detail and whether it is colour or black and grey. I estimate it after reviewing the project.

Can several subjects work in one sleeve?

Yes, if they share a clear hierarchy, compatible lighting and transitions. The composition should read as one image system.

How much does it cost?

My hourly rate is £140. Large projects are quoted as an estimated number of hours or sessions after assessment.

Planning a sleeve or larger piece?

Send the idea, placement, approximate coverage and references. I will review the project before offering a consultation or date.
