How to judge a healed tattoo

Start with the image, not the pores.

Zooming into tiny detail is tempting. At normal viewing distance, these four things tell you more about whether a realism tattoo still works.

01

Focal point

You should still understand what the main subject is immediately. Detail cannot rescue a composition with no clear priority.

02

Value separation

Dark, middle and light areas need enough separation for the image to keep depth instead of collapsing into one similar tone.

03

Edges

Some edges should remain firm and others soft. If every contour is equally sharp, realism can become flat; if everything is soft, the subject loses structure.

04

Breathing room

Negative space and quieter passages stop dense realism from becoming a wall of information as the tattoo settles.

Healed Tattoo FAQ

Why are healed tattoo photos useful?+

Fresh tattoos can look darker, brighter and glossier immediately after a session. A confirmed healed photograph gives a better view of settled contrast, colour relationships and overall readability.

Is every tattoo in the main portfolio healed?+

No. The main portfolio contains completed tattoo photography, but this page only labels an image as healed when the source explicitly confirms it.

What should I look for in healed realism?+

Look at the large shapes first: whether the focal subject is still clear, whether dark and light areas remain separated and whether the composition reads at normal viewing distance. Fine detail should sit inside that structure.

Does a fresh tattoo look the same after healing?+

No. Fresh work has temporary surface effects from the tattooing process, so colour, black values and highlights can appear different once the skin has settled.
