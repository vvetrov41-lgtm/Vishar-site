The reference matters

Likeness starts before the needle.

For a portrait, I need enough information in the source image to understand the facial structure. Clear eyes, a readable mouth, natural light and an expression that actually feels like the person are more useful than aggressive filters or artificial sharpening.

If you have several photographs, send them all. One may be best for the expression, another for hair or a detail that is lost in the main image. I can assess what is usable before the project is booked.

Portraits can be standalone pieces or part of a larger realism composition.

Useful

Clear facial information

High-resolution photos where the important facial structures are visible without heavy compression.

Useful

Directional light

Light and shadow describe the face. Flat flash can remove much of the form that makes a portrait convincing.

Avoid

Tiny screenshots

If the original photograph exists, send it rather than a screenshot from social media or a messaging app.

Avoid

Heavy beauty filters

Filters can erase skin transitions and alter features. A natural source gives me more reliable information to work from.

Scale

Enough room for the face

I will recommend a larger size when eyelashes, lips, nostrils and tonal transitions would otherwise be compressed into an area that cannot hold them clearly.

Placement

Keep key features off bad folds

Eyes and mouths need stable placement. I consider how the body bends and rotates before deciding the final crop and orientation.

Background

Support the expression

Background elements should frame the portrait, not compete with it. On larger projects I use transitions and negative space to control where the eye lands first.

Portrait Tattoo FAQ

What makes a good reference photo?+

Enough resolution to see the eyes, nose, mouth and skin transitions clearly. Directional light and a natural expression are more useful than a heavily filtered or compressed image.

Can you work from an old photograph?+

Sometimes. I need to assess the original image first. If important facial information is missing, I will explain that limitation rather than invent details that could change the likeness.

Is colour or black and grey better for portraits?+

Both can work. Black and grey emphasises tonal structure and light. Colour can reproduce skin tones, clothing and environmental colour. The reference and surrounding composition usually make the decision clearer.

How small can a realistic portrait be?+

There is no useful universal minimum. If the face is too small to keep the important structures readable, I will recommend increasing the scale or simplifying the composition.

How much does a portrait tattoo cost?+

My hourly rate is £140. I estimate the time after reviewing the reference, placement, intended size, skin and whether the portrait is standalone or part of a larger composition.
