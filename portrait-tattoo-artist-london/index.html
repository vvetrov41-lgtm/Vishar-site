The reference matters

Likeness starts before the needle.

For a portrait, I need enough information in the source image to understand the facial structure. Clear eyes, a readable mouth, natural light and an expression that actually feels like the person are more useful than aggressive filters or artificial sharpening.

If you have several photographs, send them all. One may be best for the expression, another for hair or a detail that is lost in the main image. I can assess what is usable before the project is booked.

Portraits can be standalone pieces or part of a larger realism composition.

Useful

Clear facial information

High-resolution photos where the eyes, nose, mouth and important facial structures are visible without heavy compression.

Useful

Directional light

Light and shadow describe the face. A reference with clear form gives me more reliable information to translate to skin.

Avoid

Tiny screenshots

If the original photograph exists, send it rather than a screenshot from social media or a messaging app.

Avoid

Heavy beauty filters

Filters can erase skin transitions and alter features. A natural source gives me better information to work from.

Portrait Tattoo FAQ

What makes a good portrait reference?

Enough resolution to read the facial structure, with natural detail and useful light. Original files are better than screenshots.

Can you work from an old photograph?

Sometimes. I assess what information is still present and explain any limitations before booking.

How small can a portrait be?

If the size is too small to keep the eyes, nose, mouth and expression readable, I will recommend a larger scale or simpler composition.

How much does a portrait cost?

My hourly rate is £140. Time depends on the reference, placement, size, skin and surrounding composition.

Have a portrait reference?

Send the original photo if possible, plus placement and approximate size. I will review whether the reference is strong enough before the project is booked.
